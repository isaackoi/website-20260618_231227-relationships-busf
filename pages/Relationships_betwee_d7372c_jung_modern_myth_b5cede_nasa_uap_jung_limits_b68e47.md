## Introduction

Carl Jung’s famous claim that [flying saucers]({{ 'flying-saucer/' | relative_url }}) function as a modern myth faces an important test in the era of [Unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) research. NASA’s recent approach to UAPs neither confirms alien visitors nor dismisses unusual reports outright. Instead, it insists on a careful distinction: an observation can be unexplained without being extraterrestrial. That distinction matters because Jung’s argument was never primarily about spacecraft. It was about the meanings that societies attach to uncertain phenomena.

Modern UAP science therefore creates a useful boundary. NASA asks what can be established through sensors, data quality and transparent investigation. Jung asked why certain images, rumours and expectations become culturally powerful. Rather than cancelling one another out, these approaches reveal that scientific explanation and symbolic interpretation address different questions. NASA’s caution tests Jung’s theory by removing easy assumptions and forcing attention to what remains when mystery is separated from evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director - NASASeptember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why Unidentified Does Not Mean Extraterrestrial

One of the central features of NASA’s UAP programme is its refusal to treat “unidentified” as a synonym for “alien”. The agency defines UAP as observations that cannot presently be identified as known aircraft or natural phenomena. The purpose of investigation is to improve data collection and analysis, not to validate a preferred explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA Science...</span></span></span>

This position directly challenges a common cultural pattern that Jung would have recognised. In periods of uncertainty, people often fill gaps in knowledge with narratives that provide coherence. During the classic flying-saucer era of the 1950s, unexplained lights or objects were frequently interpreted through the imagery of space-age [hopes and fears]({{ 'hopes-and-fears/' | relative_url }}). The same tendency persists today. Public discussion often jumps from “we do not know what this is” to “it may be an alien craft”.

NASA’s caution interrupts that leap. Its independent study concluded that current evidence does not demonstrate an extraterrestrial origin for reported UAPs, while also acknowledging that some cases remain unresolved because available data are inadequate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director - NASASeptember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2023</span></span></span>

For Jung’s interpretation, this is a revealing moment. If unexplained observations continue to generate powerful alien narratives even when scientific institutions explicitly refuse that conclusion, then the cultural and psychological dimensions he emphasised remain relevant. The persistence of meaning-making becomes a subject of study in its own right.

## What Better Data Can and Cannot Settle

### NASA’s governance model

NASA’s UAP effort is structured around a governance problem: how should institutions investigate unusual claims without sensationalism, [secrecy]({{ 'secrecy/' | relative_url }}) or premature conclusions? The agency’s recommendations focus on improving sensor quality, reducing [reporting stigma]({{ 'reporting-stigma/' | relative_url }}), increasing transparency and developing methods capable of distinguishing genuine anomalies from ordinary misidentifications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director - NASASeptember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2023</span></span></span>

This approach reflects a basic scientific principle. Better observations can resolve questions about physical events. A sharper image, calibrated sensors or corroborating measurements may show whether an object was a balloon, atmospheric effect, drone or something else entirely.

### The limits of empirical resolution

Yet even perfect data cannot answer every question that interested Jung.

Suppose future investigations identify the causes of most reported UAP incidents. Such findings could reduce the number of unexplained cases, but they would not automatically explain why UFO stories became culturally compelling. The symbolic power of flying [saucers]({{ 'saucers/' | relative_url }}), alien visitors and cosmic surveillance is not identical to the physical reality of any specific sighting.

The distinction resembles the relationship between astronomy and astrology. Astronomy can determine the motions of planets. It cannot by itself explain why humans repeatedly invest celestial objects with symbolic meaning. Likewise, UAP science can evaluate observations, but it does not fully explain why certain interpretations gain emotional force and spread through society.

NASA’s programme therefore functions as a practical test of Jung’s framework. If improved data solve individual cases while public fascination remains strong, then the symbolic dimension survives independently of the empirical outcome.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director - NASASeptember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2023</span></span></span>

## How Mythic Meaning Survives Scientific Caution

A common misunderstanding is that myths disappear when science advances. Jung’s argument was subtler. Myths are not merely incorrect [explanations]({{ 'explanations/' | relative_url }}). They are structures of meaning that help people organise uncertainty, fear and hope.

The current UAP debate illustrates this distinction clearly. Modern discussions are shaped by military sensors, satellite technology, government reports and scientific review. Yet many of the recurring themes resemble those found in earlier flying-saucer culture:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* Hidden knowledge withheld from the public.
* Superior intelligences watching humanity.
* Warnings about technological danger.
* Expectations of salvation, intervention or revelation.
* Suspicion that official authorities know more than they admit.

</div>

These themes persist even when investigators emphasise methodological caution. NASA’s effort to move discussion away from sensational claims and toward evidence-based inquiry shows how difficult it is to separate observation from interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/nasa-ufos-aliens-report-2023" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The agency stressed the need to shift the conversation from sensationalism to science and eliminate the stigma associated with reporting...</span></span></span>

From Jung’s perspective, this persistence is significant. The myth does not depend on proof of alien visitation. It depends on the continuing cultural role played by the idea of the visitor from elsewhere. In a technologically advanced society, extraterrestrials often occupy symbolic territory once associated with angels, omens or divine messengers. The imagery changes; the psychological function may remain recognisable.

## UAPs, Science Fiction and the Modern Imagination

The connection to science fiction becomes especially visible here. Science fiction has long supplied the visual vocabulary through which people imagine advanced civilisations, mysterious craft and contact with non-human intelligence. In turn, public discussions of UFOs and UAPs often draw on those same images.

NASA’s cautious language creates a contrast with science-fiction narratives. Scientific investigation treats unidentified events as data problems. Science fiction treats them as story opportunities. The gap between those approaches helps explain why UFO culture has repeatedly absorbed fictional motifs without requiring factual confirmation.

This does not mean that all UFO reports originate from fiction. Rather, fiction provides ready-made frameworks for interpreting ambiguity. When an observation lacks a clear explanation, culturally familiar narratives compete to fill the void. Jung’s emphasis on symbols and collective imagination remains useful precisely because scientific caution leaves that interpretive space open.

## Where Jung Ends and UAP Science Begins

NASA’s UAP programme establishes a boundary that Jung himself would likely have recognised. Questions about what happened in the sky belong to empirical investigation. Questions about why certain explanations become compelling belong to cultural and psychological analysis.

The agency’s insistence on evidence, transparency and methodological restraint does not invalidate Jung’s modern-myth interpretation. Instead, it clarifies its proper domain. Better sensors may determine whether a particular sighting has an ordinary explanation. They cannot by themselves explain why images of mysterious craft, hidden intelligences and cosmic visitors continue to capture public imagination.

In that sense, NASA’s caution is less a refutation of Jung than a test of his central insight. The more carefully institutions separate unexplained observations from claims about aliens, the more clearly the distinction emerges between physical evidence and symbolic meaning. UAP science investigates the phenomenon. Jung’s myth reading investigates what people make of it.
